## What Problems Require Emergency Dental Treatment?

Not every dental problem requires immediate treatment, but some symptoms should never be ignored. Common reasons to seek urgent dental attention include:

- **Severe toothache:** Persistent or throbbing pain may indicate deep decay, inflammation inside the tooth or an infection.
- **Broken or cracked teeth:** Accidents, sports injuries and biting hard foods can damage teeth and expose sensitive inner tissue.
- **Dental abscesses:** An infection can cause pus, swelling, tenderness and an unpleasant taste in the mouth.
- **Knocked-out teeth:** A permanent tooth that has been completely displaced requires immediate attention.
- **Lost fillings or crowns:** A damaged restoration can expose the underlying tooth and cause discomfort.
- **Bleeding after a dental injury:** Bleeding that does not stop with appropriate pressure needs prompt assessment.

A dental examination helps determine the cause of the problem and whether the tooth can be preserved. Depending on the diagnosis, treatment may include a filling, crown, root canal procedure, drainage of an infection or extraction.

## How Do You Know If a Toothache Is a Dental Emergency?

A mild toothache may have several causes, but persistent pain, facial swelling, pain when biting or sensitivity that interferes with daily activities deserves professional attention.

Additional warning signs include fever, pus near the gums, swelling around the jaw and difficulty opening the mouth. These symptoms can indicate an infection that requires prompt treatment.

**Important:** Difficulty breathing or swallowing, rapidly spreading facial or neck swelling, or swelling that threatens the airway requires immediate emergency medical care. Serious facial injuries and uncontrolled bleeding may also require hospital treatment.

## What Treatments Are Available for Dental Emergencies?

The appropriate treatment depends on the extent of the damage, the location of the affected tooth and the patient's oral health.

**Tooth decay:** A dentist may remove decayed tissue and restore the tooth with a filling or crown. If decay reaches the pulp, root canal treatment may help preserve the natural tooth.

**Dental infections:** Treatment may involve draining an abscess, performing root canal therapy or removing a tooth that cannot be saved. Antibiotics may be necessary in certain cases, but they do not replace treatment of the underlying source.

**Broken teeth:** Small fractures may be repaired with bonding material or a filling, while more extensive damage may require a crown or another procedure.

**Knocked-out permanent teeth:** Time is particularly important. Hold the tooth by its crown rather than the root, avoid scrubbing it and keep it moist in milk or a suitable tooth-preservation solution while seeking immediate dental advice. Do not force the tooth back into its socket.

An early examination can help establish which option is suitable and whether further treatment is needed.

## What Can You Do Before Your Dental Appointment?

If you cannot receive immediate treatment, a few simple steps may help manage discomfort temporarily.

- Rinse gently with warm salt water if you can do so comfortably.
- Apply a cold compress to the outside of your cheek to help reduce swelling.
- Eat soft foods and avoid chewing on the affected side.
- Use over-the-counter pain relief according to the label, provided it is safe for you.
- Keep the area clean and avoid touching the affected tooth with sharp objects.

Never place aspirin directly against the tooth or gums, as it can damage soft tissue. Do not attempt to drain an abscess yourself or take leftover antibiotics without professional advice.

These measures may provide temporary relief, but they cannot repair a damaged tooth or reliably resolve an infection. Contact a dental professional if symptoms persist or worsen.
